Research agenda

My research examines how political organisations build authority, interpret risk, and adapt during and after armed conflict. It connects the study of civil wars and rebel governance with research on institutional crisis recognition and the political consequences of food insecurity.

Research strands

Armed organisations and political order

I study how armed groups construct political institutions, share power, and move from wartime governance into post-conflict politics. This work focuses on Kurdish politics, North-East Syria, and Sri Lanka.

Political risk and early warning

I examine why institutions recognise some crises but miss others, with particular attention to knowledge filters and organisational blind spots. The principal cases are Sri Lanka, Israel, and Palestine.

Food security and conflict

I analyse how state and non-state actors use control over food and how food insecurity affects political instability, migration, conflict prevention, and post-conflict peacebuilding, especially in the Middle East and North Africa.

Cases and methods

My research combines comparative case analysis with interviews, field observation, documentary evidence, and conflict data. I have conducted fieldwork in Turkey, Iraq’s Kurdistan Region, and Sri Lanka.
